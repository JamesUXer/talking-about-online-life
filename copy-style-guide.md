# Copy Style Guide: Talking About Online Life

Use this guide before writing or accepting copy for the prototype.

## Voice Guide

Audience:
Busy primary staff with mixed confidence about online life. The copy should never make staff feel behind, blamed or tested.

Tone:
Calm, human, curious, non-alarmist.

Sentence style:
Short to medium sentences. Plain English. No policy drag. Each section should feel like staff coaching, not compliance training.

Avoid:
- "you must"
- "ensure"
- "digital risks"
- "red flags"
- "failure to"
- guilt-based language
- technical jargon
- long safeguarding explanations

Prefer:
- "try"
- "notice"
- "a small moment"
- "could"
- "one pupil"
- "low-pressure"
- "keep it light"
- "when it feels natural"

## Copy Rubric

Before accepting a section, check:

- Does it sound like staff coaching, not compliance?
- Does it reduce pressure rather than add it?
- Is the child framed as a whole person, not a risk case?
- Is the action concrete and realistic in a school day?
- Is there progression from noticing, to trying, to reflecting?
- Could an older, less tech-confident staff member feel respected by it?

## Progression

Week 1:
Notice online life as part of ordinary life.

Week 2:
Use small, repeated conversations to build trust.

Week 3:
Respond calmly when an online moment appears.

Week 4:
Make talking about online life part of everyday school culture.
