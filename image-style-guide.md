# Image Style Guide: Talking About Online Life

Use this guide when creating or selecting imagery for the prototype.

The aim is to add warmth and child-perspective texture without making the CPD feel childish. Imagery should support the staff-coaching tone: calm, curious, low-pressure and human.

## Core Image Direction

Create a small, consistent image set:

- 1 homepage or Week 1 hero image
- 4 small storyboard paper drawings
- 1 reflection or action image

Visual style:
The visual world is a UK primary exercise book. Use off-white lined paper, faint blue rules, a subtle red margin, slight paper creases and soft classroom light. Drawings should look like sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip marks sitting on the page. Add a second, adult layer through gentle pencil circles, ticks, underlines or brackets, as if a staff member has noticed one small moment.

The image should feel like child perspective held inside a professional adult CPD resource. It should be distinctive enough to belong to this app, but quiet enough to leave space for the learning.

Avoid:

- identifiable child faces
- real pupil work
- school names, logos or uniforms
- app logos or platform names
- warning signs, locks, hackers, code screens or threat imagery
- glossy stock illustration
- cartoon mascots
- bright childish classroom-display styling
- fear, blame or urgency

Prefer:

- lined exercise-book paper
- a red margin line
- paper texture
- adult pencil annotation marks
- hand-drawn speech bubbles
- simple classroom objects
- ordinary play, conversation and curiosity
- small online-life clues, such as a game controller, headphones or tablet outline
- wide empty space
- natural shadows
- calm negative space

## Prompt Rubric

Score each generated image from 1 to 5 for each criterion.

### 1. Tone Fit

5: Calm, adult-facing, warm and non-alarmist. Feels suitable for primary staff CPD.  
3: Pleasant but a little generic, decorative or classroom-display-like.  
1: Too childish, too corporate, too dramatic or too cyber-safety coded.

### 2. Child-Perspective Without Identifiable Children

5: Suggests children's lived experience through objects, drawings or marks, with no faces or personal details.  
3: Child-friendly, but the perspective is unclear or too adult-designed.  
1: Shows identifiable children, personal work, names, uniforms or disclosure-like content.

### 3. Professional Restraint

5: Adds texture and meaning while leaving room for the learning content.  
3: Visually appealing but slightly busy or decorative.  
1: Dominates the page or makes the resource feel like a children's activity.

### 4. Relevance To The Learning Moment

5: Clearly supports everyday online-life conversation, curiosity and trust.  
3: Related to school or online life, but not specific to the learning moment.  
1: Feels like generic online safety, generic school stock, or unrelated decoration.

### 5. Consistency Across The Set

5: Matches the same paper, line, lighting, crop and accent-colour language as the rest of the set.  
3: Similar style, but noticeable variation in line weight, colour or realism.  
1: Looks like a separate illustration system.

### 6. Safeguarding Sensitivity

5: No fear cues, no platform naming, no sensitive scenario, no accidental child disclosure.  
3: Safe overall, but includes a detail that could distract or imply a risk scenario.  
1: Uses alarmist symbols, real brands, personal details or unsafe implications.

## Acceptance Threshold

Only use an image if it scores:

- 26 or above out of 30 overall
- 5 for Safeguarding Sensitivity
- 4 or above for Tone Fit
- 4 or above for Professional Restraint

Reject the image if any of these appear:

- identifiable child face
- real or fake school logo
- app or platform logo
- padlock, hacker, code, warning triangle or cyber-threat visual
- readable personal names, messages or usernames
- dramatic fear-based lighting
- overly cute cartoon style

## Iteration Process

1. Generate 3 to 5 versions from the same prompt.
2. Score each version with the rubric.
3. Keep only the strongest 1 or 2.
4. Write a short revision note for the next generation, naming only what needs changing.
5. Regenerate with the same core art direction plus the revision note.
6. Repeat until the image reaches the acceptance threshold.
7. Crop and place the image in the interface only after the set feels consistent.

Do not accept the first image just because it is attractive. It needs to fit the learning tone.

## Master Prompt

Use this as the base for all generated images:

> UK primary exercise-book page, off-white lined paper with faint blue ruled lines, a subtle red margin, slight paper creases and soft natural classroom light. Sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip drawings sit low on the page, with lots of quiet empty space. Add gentle adult pencil annotation marks: a loose circle, underline, tick or bracket. Child perspective with adult restraint, calm staff CPD tone, distinctive notebook world. No faces, no children, no identifiable people, no app logos, no school logos, no readable text, no letters or numbers, no usernames, no warning symbols, no padlocks, no hackers, no code, no cyber-threat imagery, no glossy vector style, no cartoon mascot.

## Hero Image Prompt

> Wide 16:9 hero image. UK primary exercise-book page with faint blue ruled lines, subtle red margin, off-white paper texture, slight creases and soft classroom light. Sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip drawings arranged low across the page: blank speech bubbles, headphones, a controller, a blank tablet outline, a lunchbox and a school chair. Add gentle adult pencil ticks or circles. Lots of empty paper space. Calm, human, low-pressure staff CPD tone. No faces, no children, no logos, no readable text, no warning imagery, no padlocks, no hackers, no code, no mascot.

## Storyboard Prompts

Use the same style for all four storyboard images.

### Panel 1: The Comment

> Square storyboard panel. UK primary exercise-book page with faint blue ruled lines, subtle red margin, off-white paper texture and soft classroom light. Sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip drawing low on the page: a school desk edge, a blank tablet outline, a lunchbox, headphones and two blank speech bubbles. Add one soft adult pencil circle around the small moment. Calm and ordinary. No people, no faces, no readable text, no logos.

### Panel 2: The Unknown Platform

> Square storyboard panel. UK primary exercise-book page with faint blue ruled lines, subtle red margin, off-white paper texture and soft classroom light. Sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip drawing low on the page: a blank tablet outline with abstract unbranded shapes, a loose winding doodle path, headphones and a pencil. Add a calm adult pencil bracket near the tablet. The feeling is curious, not worried. No people, no faces, no readable text, no logos, no warning imagery.

### Panel 3: The Adult Moment

> Square storyboard panel. UK primary exercise-book page with faint blue ruled lines, subtle red margin, off-white paper texture and soft classroom light. Sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip drawing low on the page: a classroom table edge, an adult pencil, a small blank sticky note, a cup and a plain unbranded tablet outline. Add a gentle pencil circle around a tiny blank speech bubble to show noticing. No people, no faces, no readable text, no logos, no warning imagery.

### Panel 4: The Opening

> Square storyboard panel. UK primary exercise-book page with faint blue ruled lines, subtle red margin, off-white paper texture and soft classroom light. Sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip drawing low on the page: two empty speech bubbles, headphones, a blank tablet outline and a school jumper sleeve or desk edge. Add one adult pencil tick and a light underline to suggest a low-pressure opening. No people, no faces, no readable text, no logos, no warning imagery.

## Reflection Or Action Image Prompt

> Wide reflection image. UK primary exercise-book page with faint blue ruled lines, subtle red margin, off-white paper texture, slight creases and soft classroom light. Sparse childlike pencil and felt-tip drawing across the lower third: an adult pencil beside a blank sticky note, a soft circled blank speech bubble, headphones, a plain unbranded tablet outline and a small school chair. Add a gentle underline, one small tick and one loose circle. It should suggest trying one small question this week without visible words. No people, no faces, no readable text, no logos, no warning imagery.

## Placement Guidance

Hero:
Use the strongest image as a large supporting visual. Keep text readable and do not place important text over busy drawing areas.

Storyboard:
Use small crops or fragments. The image should support each panel, not replace the text.

Reflection/action:
Use as a quiet visual cue near the action card or reflection area. Keep it secondary to the prompt.

## Final Quality Check

Before adding any image to the site, ask:

- Would a busy teacher understand the feeling in two seconds?
- Does it make the page feel more human without making it childish?
- Does it avoid fear, guilt and surveillance?
- Does it support one small, realistic conversation?
- Does it look like part of the same image family as the others?
