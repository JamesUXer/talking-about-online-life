Week 3

Spotting natural openings

Children often mention online life in passing. You do not need a script or expert knowledge of the platform. One calm question can help you understand what the moment means to them.

Week 2 focused on making online life normal to talk about. This week is about what to do when an opening appears.

Small moments can become safe conversations.

A useful pattern

Open the door, then listen

The aim is not to turn every comment into a lesson. Notice the moment, ask one useful question and listen to what comes next.

  1. 1

    Notice

    Hear the online-life cue without assuming what it means.

  2. 2

    Ask

    Use one calm, open question that fits the moment.

  3. 3

    Listen

    Give the child room to explain without blame or surprise.

  4. 4

    Act

    Use the school safeguarding procedure if a concern appears.

You are opening a conversation, not trying to solve the whole situation on the spot.

Practise

Is this a natural opening?

Read each moment and choose the response that best keeps the door open. You can change your answer after reading the feedback.

Moment 1 of 5

Before registration

“I was up really late playing last night.”

What would you say first?

A useful opening. This asks for context without assuming that the game is the problem. Listen to what the child says next. Tiredness may be a one-off or part of a wider pattern.

This moves quickly to judgement and may close the conversation. Understanding what happened gives you more useful context.

Online experiences can affect sleep, mood, friendships and learning in school. A brief follow-up is reasonable even when the activity happened elsewhere.

During a classroom conversation

“That video was weird but funny.”

Which response keeps the tone open?

A useful opening. This gently explores the child’s reaction without suggesting that they have done something wrong.

The adult still does not know what the child saw or how it affected them. A warning at this point may replace useful listening.

Asking to inspect content can increase pressure and is not the default first step. Start with the child’s account. If a concern emerges, follow the school’s safeguarding procedure.

A passing comment

“I blocked someone yesterday.”

What is the most useful first response?

A useful opening. Blocking may have been sensible, but the comment could also point to conflict, pressure or unwanted contact. Ask once, listen and follow the school’s safeguarding route if anything feels concerning.

Blocking may not be the end of the situation. This could close the conversation before the child explains why they acted.

Staff should not begin their own online investigation. Listen to the child and use the school’s established reporting process.

Talking about a game

“I made a new friend in my game.”

Which response shows calm curiosity?

A useful opening. This invites context without immediately treating the friendship as suspicious. What the child says next may reassure you or suggest a concern that needs passing on.

This dismisses something that may matter to the child and could make them less likely to talk about it again.

This becomes an investigation and asks for information the child may not know. Begin with the child’s experience and follow safeguarding procedures if needed.

Other pupils are nearby

“I do not want to show you my game chat. It is private.”

What would be the most proportionate response?

Children can have a reasonable wish for privacy. Pressure may close the conversation and could expose private material unnecessarily.

A useful opening. This respects privacy while leaving the door open. If the child wants to talk, move to an appropriate setting, listen and follow the school’s safeguarding procedure if a concern emerges.

Respecting privacy does not require closing the door. One low-pressure check gives the child a chance to say whether something is wrong.

Choose

One question is enough to begin

The useful responses stayed calm, asked for context and left room for the child to decide what to say next.

Choose one phrase for this week

Which one could you use naturally?

Keep it light. Try the phrase once when an online-life moment appears.

Safeguarding

When the conversation becomes a concern

Stay calm and listen. Do not promise to keep the information secret and do not begin your own investigation. Follow your school’s child protection policy and report the concern to the DSL or deputy without delay.

A calm question can open the door. Once a concern appears, the school’s safeguarding procedure takes over.
